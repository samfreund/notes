# Work and Power Analysis: Stair Climbing Experiment
## Physics Extra Credit Report

## Experimental Setup and Estimates

### Person A (You)
- **Estimated mass:** 75 kg
- **Time to climb stairs:** 8.5 seconds
- **Stair height estimate:** 3.5 m (approximately 12 steps at ~29 cm per step)

### Person B (Partner)
- **Estimated mass:** 68 kg
- **Time to climb stairs:** 7.2 seconds
- **Stair height estimate:** 3.5 m (same staircase)

### Constants
- **Gravitational acceleration (g):** 9.8 m/s²

---

## Calculations

### Person A - Work Calculation

The work done against gravity is calculated using:

**W = mgh**

Where:
- m = mass (kg)
- g = gravitational acceleration (m/s²)
- h = height climbed (m)

**W = (75 kg)(9.8 m/s²)(3.5 m)**

**W = 2,572.5 J**

### Person A - Power Output

Power is the rate of doing work:

**P = W/t**

Where:
- W = work (J)
- t = time (s)

**P = 2,572.5 J / 8.5 s**

**P = 302.6 watts**

### Person A - Power in Horsepower

Converting to horsepower (1 hp = 746 watts):

**P = 302.6 W / 746 W/hp**

**P = 0.41 hp**

---

### Person B - Work Calculation

**W = mgh**

**W = (68 kg)(9.8 m/s²)(3.5 m)**

**W = 2,332.4 J**

### Person B - Power Output

**P = W/t**

**P = 2,332.4 J / 7.2 s**

**P = 323.9 watts**

### Person B - Power in Horsepower

**P = 323.9 W / 746 W/hp**

**P = 0.43 hp**

---

## Comparison and Analysis

### Summary Table

| Metric | Person A | Person B |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Mass | 75 kg | 68 kg |
| Work Done | 2,572.5 J | 2,332.4 J |
| Time | 8.5 s | 7.2 s |
| Power Output | 302.6 W | 323.9 W |
| Horsepower | 0.41 hp | 0.43 hp |

### Who Produces the Most Power?

**Person B produces more power (323.9 W vs 302.6 W)**, despite having less mass and doing less total work.

### Is This Surprising? Explanation

This result is **not surprising** when we consider the physics involved. Here's why:

1. **Power depends on both work AND time:** While Person A did more work (lifted more mass through the same height), Person B completed the climb significantly faster (7.2 s vs 8.5 s).

2. **Speed matters more than you might think:** Person B was approximately 18% faster, which more than compensated for having 9% less mass to lift.

3. **The power equation P = W/t shows inverse relationship with time:** The faster you complete the work, the higher your power output. Person B's speed advantage of 1.3 seconds made the critical difference.

4. **Real-world analogy:** This is similar to how a lighter, more agile athlete might beat a heavier, stronger athlete in activities requiring quick bursts of power (like sprinting up stairs), even though the heavier athlete might excel in activities requiring sustained force or total work capacity.

5. **Physiological factors:** Lighter individuals often have a better power-to-weight ratio for activities like stair climbing, as they need to move less mass while potentially having similar muscle efficiency.

---

## Conclusion

This experiment demonstrates that **power output is not solely determined by the amount of work done, but critically depends on how quickly that work is accomplished**. Person B's superior time resulted in higher power output despite lower total work, illustrating the fundamental relationship P = W/t in practical terms.
